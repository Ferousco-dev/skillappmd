## §A — The two findings this phase produced

Both come from analysing **cause**, which is the whole difference between counting defects and
learning from them.

### 1. Fixtures were the dominant defect source — 3 of 8, all HIGH or MEDIUM

`DEF-002`, `DEF-003`, `DEF-005`. In every case I wrote test data in the shape I had implemented, so
the tests agreed with the code and **both were wrong together**. Each passed a full unit suite.

The control that worked was **grading against data the project did not create** — the GitSkills
corpus and its `dedup_primary` and `frontmatter_valid` oracles. No quantity of additional
self-written testing would have substituted, because the flaw was in the shared assumption, not in
the coverage.

**50% of all defects (4 of 8) were found by real data that no fixture would have exposed.**

### 2. Planning omission caused *both* gate failures

Neither G4 nor G5 failed on code quality. Both failed because a requirement was fully specified in
the architecture and **never assigned to an increment**:

- `ARCHITECTURE.md` §5 listed raw storage as a Phase 1 subsystem → absent at G4
- `SOURCE_CONNECTORS.md` §4 specified `SkillsMPConnector` in detail → absent at G5

**The design was right both times. The plan lost it.** That is a more useful conclusion than
"two gates failed", and it points at a fix: the remedy is now mechanical rather than attentional —
`packages/tools/src/traceability.js` runs in CI and reports any requirement without a test.
`DEF-008` would have surfaced in minutes instead of weeks.

## §C — Independence: the honest finding

Three criteria across three gates have now been waived for the same reason — G4 criterion 9 (peer
review), G5 criterion 11 (independent test team), G7 criterion 5 (independent audit).

**This project's largest process weakness is that every check but one was performed by whoever did
the work.**

The exception is the counterexample worth studying. At G1 the stakeholder reversed `DEC-018` and
`DEC-019` — decisions no test I could write would have caught, because both were *consistent
implementations of a wrong idea*. One review by someone who did not build it produced two findings
that mattered more than anything automation found.

Compensating controls are mechanical and therefore genuinely independent of my judgement on a given
day: a lint that fails the build, oracles from a corpus we did not create, contract suites across
adapters sharing nothing but an interface, and a traceability checker that reports orphans whether
or not anyone wants to see them.
